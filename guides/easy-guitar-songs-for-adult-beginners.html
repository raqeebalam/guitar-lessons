12 Easy Guitar Songs for Adult Beginners (Only 2–4 Chords)

By Raqeeb Alam · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

Most "easy songs" lists are not easy. They include an F barre chord in the bridge or a strumming pattern that takes months. This list is different: every song below uses only open chords, none needs a barre, and they are sorted from genuinely easiest to slightly harder. Put a capo on if the original key is hard to sing; the shapes stay the same.

Level 1: Two chords

1. "Horse With No Name" – America

Chords: Em, D6/9 (a two-finger shape). Tip: The whole song is these two shapes back and forth. Perfect first song for practising a relaxed down-down-up strum.

2. "Jambalaya" – Hank Williams

Chords: C, G. Tip: Classic country two-chord song. Play one down-strum per beat until the change is smooth, then add the up-strums.

3. "Eleanor Rigby" – The Beatles

Chords: Em, C. Tip: Only two chords, but the changes are quick. Great for building speed between two shapes you already know.

Level 2: Three chords

4. "Bad Moon Rising" – Creedence Clearwater Revival

Chords: D, A, G. Tip: The most-taught three-chord song for a reason. Fast tempo, so start at half speed.

5. "Ring of Fire" – Johnny Cash

Chords: G, C, D. Tip: Steady boom-chick country strum: bass note, then chord. Sounds instantly like the record.

6. "Three Little Birds" – Bob Marley

Chords: A, D, E. Tip: Slow reggae feel. Mute the strings with your palm between strums for the off-beat sound.

7. "Love Me Do" – The Beatles

Chords: G, C, D. Tip: Same chords as Ring of Fire in a different order. Learn both and you have proof you can reuse shapes.

8. "Sweet Home Alabama" – Lynyrd Skynyrd

Chords: D, C, G. Tip: Ignore the lead riff for now and strum the chords. The riff comes in month three.

Level 3: Four chords (the "everything" progression)

These songs use G, D, Em, C or a close variation. Learn this one progression and you unlock hundreds of pop songs.

9. "Let It Be" – The Beatles

Chords: C, G, Am, F (play F as an easy Fmaj7 for now). Tip: Slow, forgiving tempo; ideal first four-chord song.

10. "Knockin' on Heaven's Door" – Bob Dylan

Chords: G, D, Am, C. Tip: Two bars per chord, so you have time to change. Add the down-down-up-up-down-up pattern once the changes are clean.

11. "Riptide" – Vance Joy

Chords: Am, G, C (F appears briefly; Fmaj7 works). Tip: Fast strum but the same three shapes throughout. Popular with students in their 20s and 30s.

12. "Wonderwall" – Oasis

Chords: Em7, G, Dsus4, A7sus4 (capo 2). Tip: Every one of these is a two- or three-finger shape and your ring and pinky fingers never move. Sounds far harder than it is, which is exactly what you want at a party.

For South Asian students: easy Bollywood and Coke Studio songs

  • "Kun Faya Kun" – Am, G, F (Fmaj7), C. Slow, gorgeous, forgiving.
  • "Tum Hi Ho" – Am, F, C, G with a capo. The four-chord loop repeats for the whole song.
  • "Aadat" (Atif Aslam) – Em, C, G, D. Same progression as a dozen Western pop hits.
  • "Channa Mereya" – G, D, Em, C (capo). A great one for practising singing while playing.

How to actually learn one of these in a week

  1. Pick one song. Not three.
  2. Day 1–2: get each chord ringing cleanly on its own.
  3. Day 3–4: change between chords with one down-strum per bar, slowly.
  4. Day 5–6: add the strumming pattern, still slow, with the original recording at 75% speed (YouTube playback settings).
  5. Day 7: full speed with the recording. Record yourself on your phone. You will be surprised.
